# Macro Brief Style Guide
## Reference Exemplars and Rules for Morning Brief Agent

---

## EXEMPLAR 1 — Opening: Regime Signal Done Right

**Use this pattern for sentence 1. No preamble. Lead with the signal.**

> "The single most important signal of the past 30 days is the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, through which 20% of global oil supply normally transits. U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran on February 28 triggered a cascade that has pushed Brent crude from roughly $70 to a peak of $126 per barrel — an energy shock the IEA called unprecedented since the 1970s. This one event has reordered every other macro variable: reignited inflation from an already uncomfortable baseline, forced the Fed into indefinite hold, widened credit spreads at the worst possible moment for a maturing credit cycle, and broken the reflexive bid for risk assets that had sustained the bull case through early 2026."

**Why it works:**
- Single signal named in sentence 1
- Specific number with source and date
- Transmission mechanism spelled out immediately
- Every downstream section flows from this thesis

**Failure pattern to avoid:**
> "The macro environment remains complex as multiple factors compete for market attention."
This tells the reader nothing. It is not a regime call. It is noise.

## EXEMPLAR 2 — Contradiction Resolution: Liquidity Flush + Weak Risk Assets

**Use this pattern when nominal liquidity looks fine but markets are weak.**

> "The net liquidity impulse is mildly positive at the margin: the Fed is no longer draining, the RRP drain is exhausted, and TGA drawdowns during the current fiscal year inject cash. The plumbing is not the problem — it's the rate environment sitting on top of that plumbing. With the FOMC holding at 3.50–3.75% and the energy shock guaranteeing no imminent pivot, the real rate of interest is crushing rate-sensitive borrowers even as nominal liquidity appears ample."

**Why it works:**
- Acknowledges the bullish read (plumbing is fine)
- Identifies the override mechanism (real rates, not nominal liquidity)
- Does not leave the contradiction unresolved
- Produces a tradeable implication: real-rate-sensitive names remain under pressure regardless of system liquidity

**Failure pattern to avoid:**
> "Liquidity conditions are supportive but the Fed remains cautious."
This acknowledges both sides without resolving the tension. Useless.

## EXEMPLAR 3 — Cross-Asset Signal with Explicit Mechanism

**Use this pattern when an asset moves against what the narrative would predict.**

> "Gold's behavior has become the most analytically interesting cross-asset signal: gold has plunged approximately 15% from its March highs in one of the sharpest corrections in modern gold market history, driven by the hawkish FOMC, rising real yields, and dollar strength. This is not a bear market in gold — it is a paper-market liquidation cascade where the oil-inflation-rates transmission mechanism is temporarily overriding the structural debasement and central bank demand thesis. The structural bull thesis remains intact; the paper-to-physical divergence emerging between COMEX and Shanghai is a canary in the dollar system, not a signal to abandon the structural long."

**Why it works:**
- Explains why gold fell despite a war (counterintuitive → explains mechanism)
- Distinguishes temporary (paper liquidation) from structural (debasement thesis)
- Takes a clear position on the structural thesis
- Produces a tradeable implication: GLD dip is not a breakdown, structural long intact

**BTC corollary pattern:**
> "Bitcoin at $70,085 is behaving as a high-beta risk asset, not a macro hedge — it correlates with tech longs, and the current down-tape in Nasdaq is a headwind. Its correlation regime is speculative equity, not gold-equivalent. Use it as a risk-on/risk-off barometer, not as a geopolitical signal."

## EXEMPLAR 4 — Tail Risk: The One the Consensus Is Missing

**Use this pattern for the underappreciated risk in the risk matrix. Be specific. Non-obvious.**

> "The underappreciated tail risk consensus is entirely missing: Iran's alternative shipping corridor through Larak Island, with case-by-case IRGC approval, is evolving into a formalized oil settlement architecture that prices in yuan and excludes U.S. allies — not a temporary workaround but the embryonic infrastructure of a post-Petrodollar settlement system. If this corridor achieves volume scale while Hormuz remains closed, the dollar's role as the exclusive energy settlement currency faces its most direct structural challenge since Bretton Woods. That is not a 30-day risk — it is a 30-year risk with a 30-day catalytic moment."

**Why it works:**
- Specific mechanism, not vague "geopolitical uncertainty"
- Distinguishes market pricing from actual risk
- Calibrates the time horizon (30-day catalyst for a 30-year risk)
- Non-obvious: the market is focused on oil price, not petrodollar architecture

**Failure pattern to avoid:**
> "A tail risk is an unexpected escalation in the Middle East conflict."
This says nothing the market doesn't already know. It is not an underappreciated risk.

## STYLE RULES — QUICK REFERENCE

### Sentence patterns that work
- `"The [indicator] at [number] as of [date] signals [implication]."`
- `"This is not [common misread] — it is [correct interpretation]."`
- `"Markets are [overpricing/underpricing] [risk] because [mechanism]."`
- `"[Asset] fell despite [narrative expectation] because [transmission channel]."`
- `"The underappreciated tail risk: [specific non-obvious mechanism]."`

### Required vocabulary
spreads · basis · convexity · reflexivity · duration · term premium · vol-of-vol · positioning squeeze · bear steepener · credit bifurcation · gamma flip · forced liquidation · extend-and-pretend · maturity wall · velocity of widening · paper-to-physical divergence

### Forbidden constructions (resolve immediately or don't use)
- "mixed picture"
- "uncertain environment"
- "could go either way"
- "it remains to be seen"
- "complex backdrop"
- Any section that doesn't connect back to the regime thesis from section 1

## CONTRADICTION RESOLUTION GUIDE

| Tension | What to diagnose | What to state |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity flush + weak risk | Real rate cost vs nominal level | Which force dominates and why |
| Strong GDPNow + weak labor | Level vs derivative | "Low hire, low fire" = late cycle, not expansion |
| Gold falling + war risk | Real yields / dollar / liquidation | Temporary vs structural; whether thesis intact |
| BTC falling + risk-off | Correlation regime | Equity beta, not macro hedge |
| Equities high + macro deterioration | Reflexivity trap | Fragility, not confirmation |

## 30-DAY CALL TEMPLATE

The final paragraph must contain exactly three sentences:
1. **Dominant regime:** "The dominant regime for the next 30 days is [X]."
2. **Single variable to watch:** "The single variable most likely to change this call is [Y]."
3. **What changes the call:** "If [condition], the regime shifts to [Z] and the playbook inverts."

This is a position. Not a disclaimer.

---

## TRADING TRANSLATION TEMPLATE

For each ticker, use this exact structure:

```
[TICKER]: [bias] | confirms on [specific level or condition] | invalidates if [condition] | [preferred options expression]
```

**GLD note:** Always distinguish paper liquidation (temporary, structural bull intact) from structural breakdown (thesis change). State which it is.

**IBIT/BTC note:** Always state current correlation regime: equity beta or macro hedge. This determines whether it belongs in the risk-on or safe-haven bucket.

**High-beta names note:** Pull 1-2 names from current RS screener that the macro regime specifically favors. Name the regime-specific reason (energy tailwind, defense capex, GLP-1 pricing power, etc.).
